AI Strategy  ·  Framing the Conversation

Ferny is real. The next question is how big we want this to be.

We've built a working AI assistant inside WorkSuite. It knows our domain. It answers questions about a grower's operation. We own the stack. That's a head start most companies in horticulture don't have. Now we have to decide what to do with it.

1

The name on the door

7 min

Three candidates on the table. They're not interchangeable — each one is a different brand story and a different set of customer expectations.

·
Option 1

Fernie

A name with our DNA in it. Coworker, not a feature. Lives in customer conversations the way "Watson" or "Clippy" once did — for better and worse. Forces us to commit to the personality.

Reads as: a member of the team
·
Option 2

Cora

A human name without our brand baked in. Warm, friendly, neutral enough to spin off later as its own product if we want. Doesn't tie our AI's reputation to the Silver Fern brand by default.

Reads as: a friendly assistant
·
Option 3

"Chat"

Descriptive, not a personality. What GitHub Copilot Chat did. Lower expectations, easier to revise, safer when it gets something wrong. Also: surrenders the chance to own a brand here.

Reads as: a button in the corner

The hidden question: are we naming a coworker or describing a feature? Names create personality. Personalities create loyalty — and exposure when things go wrong. "Chat" carries no risk and no upside. Fernie and Cora carry both.

2

Product, feature, or tier?

7 min

This decision shapes pricing, sales motion, support, and brand architecture. Three honest options:

A. Embedded in WorkSuite

Every customer gets it. No separate price. Increases stickiness, hides the cost inside the platform. Lowest risk; ceiling on monetization.

B. Premium tier (WorkSuite Pro)

Two-tier the customer base. Creates an upgrade path and a clear value story. Also splits the roadmap and adds a sales conversation we don't have today.

C. Standalone product

Sold to customers who don't use WorkSuite — IGCs, specialty ag, even competitors' users. Different sales motion. Most upside. Most distraction.

What this isn't

A free chatbot we give away. Model calls cost real money. The economics force a choice between A, B, and C — there's no fourth option.

The overall direction

8 min

The story that holds everything else together:

  • We're not building an AI company. We're a horticulture-native operations platform with AI as the vehicle.
  • Generic AI is on every platform. Microsoft, Salesforce, NetSuite, Notion — every B2B tool has a chat panel now. That's table stakes.
  • What no one else has is forty years of domain depth and a stack we built ourselves. Ferny knows what a Stage 3 Must Go is. ChatGPT doesn't. The platform that understands what a grower actually does wins the next decade.
  • Our AI bet is a retention and depth bet, not a flashy demo. We're not racing OpenAI. We're making the only AI in horticulture that has the data, the workflows, and the relationships to be trusted.

If a grower asks ChatGPT about their nursery, they get plausible answers. If they ask Ferny, they get the right ones — because Ferny is in the data, not just talking about it.

5

AI consulting — product, service, or wedge?

7 min

Customers are starting to ask us about their AI, not just ours. Some of them want help. Some of them want to be told what's worth doing. There's revenue there. The question is what shape it takes — and how it relates to Ferny and WorkSuite.

·
Shape 1

Pure service

Bill by the hour or the project. Predictable margin, classic consulting. Easy to start. Doesn't scale past the people in the room — and pulls our best engineers off product work.

Risk: becomes the company
·
Shape 2 Likely fit

Productized engagements

Fixed-scope packages — "AI Readiness Assessment," "Implementation Sprint," "Custom Tool Build." Repeatable, sellable, doesn't depend on the same engineer being available every time.

Risk: marketing + sales motion required
·
Shape 3

Wedge into the platform

Consulting is how we get in the door. WorkSuite + Ferny is the destination. Every engagement ends with the customer on our platform, paying ongoing.

Risk: long sales cycle

How it interacts with Ferny and WorkSuite:

  • It can feed product. Every consulting engagement teaches us what customers actually need. That's the cheapest market research we'll ever do — paid for by the customer.
  • It can cannibalize product. If we'll build it custom for the right cheque, why would a customer buy the off-the-shelf version? We have to be deliberate about what stays bespoke and what becomes a feature.
  • It can be the implementation layer. Every meaningful Ferny rollout includes services — onboarding, custom tools, training. That's not a side business; that's the way customers actually get value.

The line we draw today decides what kind of company we are in two years. A SaaS company that does light services. A services firm that happens to ship software. Or a hybrid that explicitly uses one to win the other.

6

Where Ferny stands today

5 min

What's working

  • It's live inside WorkSuite. Behind a flag, but real.
  • It knows our world. Transplant lots, grades, space planning — Ferny speaks the language because it was built here.
  • It's ours. We didn't bolt on someone else's chatbot. We own every piece of the stack.
  • It already does something useful. Read the screen, answer questions, point users where they need to go.

What it isn't yet

  • It's a helper, not a worker. It tells you what to do. It doesn't do it.
  • It hasn't met a customer. Zero growers have used it.
  • It lives inside one app. Not on the Produce desktop. Not in mobile. Not in email.
  • It's a starting point. The hardest plumbing is done. Everything that matters comes next.
7

Why this conversation matters now

5 min

Every grower we sell to is already using AI for something. ChatGPT for emails. Copilot for spreadsheets. Whatever their kids showed them on their phone. They're going to expect AI inside WorkSuite the same way they expect search inside Google.

Every B2B platform on the market — Microsoft, Salesforce, NetSuite, the whole list — has shipped a chat panel in the last twelve months. Chat is table stakes. A better search bar isn't a differentiator anymore.

What we have, and what nobody else in horticulture has, is forty years of domain depth and a stack we built ourselves. Ferny knows what a Stage 3 Must Go is. ChatGPT doesn't. That's where the moat lives — if we choose to build it.

The cost of standing still

Growers are already patching together spreadsheets, guessing at capacity, scrambling schedules, and reconciling data after the kids are asleep. That's the world we sell into. If their tools don't get smarter while everyone else's do, we're the patchwork they replace — not the one they keep.

The flip side: if we get this right, we're the only platform in our space that understands what the grower actually does. That's worth more than any feature we could ship in the same quarter.

10

What we won't do

5 min
  • We won't build a general-purpose chatbot. ChatGPT exists. Our job is to be unmistakably useful inside the operations of a greenhouse — not to answer trivia.
  • We won't outsource the brain. We can use vendor models for the language layer, but the domain logic, the data, and the customer relationship stay with us.
  • We won't ship to customers until we'd put our name on it. First wrong answer in production becomes a support ticket and a trust hit.
  • We won't replace the grower. Ferny is the apprentice with a good memory. The grower is the expert. That framing is the brand promise.
